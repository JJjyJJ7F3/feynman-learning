# Feynman Diagnostic Reference

Use this reference when a learning session needs a more explicit diagnosis.

## Signals of shallow understanding

- The learner repeats textbook syntax but cannot paraphrase.
- The explanation is a list of nouns rather than a causal chain.
- Key sentences rely on "basically", "somehow", "it just", or unexplained jumps.
- The learner can state the benefit but not the mechanism.
- The learner can state the mechanism but not why it works.
- The learner cannot explain why an alternative was not chosen.
- The learner's example is identical to the source example.
- The learner treats a conditional rule as universal.
- The learner cannot predict what happens when one input or assumption changes.
- The learner can explain but cannot solve a nearby new problem.

## Useful probe families

### Definition
- What do you mean by X here?
- How is X different from Y?

### Causality
- Why does A lead to B?
- What happens between these two steps?
- Which mechanism produces this effect?

### Necessity
- Why is this step/component needed?
- What breaks if it is removed?

### Alternatives
- Why not use Y instead?
- Under what conditions would Y be better?

### Boundary
- Does this always hold?
- Which assumption makes it true?
- Give a case where it stops being useful.

### Prediction
- If we increase/decrease/change X, what happens?
- Which part of the behavior should remain unchanged?

### Transfer
- Apply the idea to a different example.
- Diagnose this incorrect explanation.
- Choose between two approaches and defend the choice.

## Feedback format

Prefer:

**Accurate:** what the learner got right.

**Gap:** one precise missing or incorrect link.

**Why it matters:** how the gap affects the model.

**Next:** one question or micro-task that lets the learner repair it.

Avoid overwhelming the learner with a long error list unless they explicitly request a full assessment.
